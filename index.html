                How it works

                The page prices every current plan on your own usage, half hour by half hour. It then ranks the plans by cost.

                Where the prices come from

                Every retailer must publish its plans in the government's public plan list. The law that requires this is the Consumer Data Right. The same list feeds Energy Made Easy and Victorian Energy Compare. Once a week, this site saves a copy of every plan on each Victorian electricity network and gas network. When you open the page, it downloads that copy for your network: one small file, so it takes a second or two. If the copy is missing or more than 8 days old, or you press "Get today's prices", the page downloads today's plans from the government's list instead, which takes about 20 seconds. It keeps the prices on your device for one day.

                Your usage

                Your usage file shows how much power your home took from the grid in each 5-minute or 30-minute interval. It also shows how much solar power your home sent out. The page reads the last 12 months of the file. For each day that is missing from the file, the page uses your average day.

                Some homes have a separate hot-water circuit with its own meter (controlled load). The file then shows that circuit's use as well. The page prices only the plans that have a price for that circuit, and adds its cost; other plans are left out, because a home with the circuit cannot use them without rewiring.

                No file? A sample home

                If you do not have your usage file, you can price plans on a sample home with 1 to 5 or more people. It is an average home, not yours. So please use it to get a feel for the plans, and use your own file when you can.

                • How much it uses in each season comes from the Australian Energy Regulator's benchmarks for Victorian homes (Frontier Economics, 2020). For example, a 2-person home uses about 4,840 kWh a year, most of it in winter.
                • When in the day it uses power comes from all homes on your network in 2024 and 2025. This is the market operator's data, published by the Essential Services Commission.
                • That average includes homes with solar panels, so the sample home uses a little less power at midday than a home without solar.
                • The seasonal figures are for Melbourne's climate. A home in a colder or hotter part of Victoria may use more.

                Your own home could use much more or much less, and at quite different times. That changes which plans come out cheapest, most of all for plans whose price changes through the day.

                How each plan is priced

                1. Each half hour of your usage costs the plan's usage rate for that time of day. Times are Melbourne times, with daylight saving. Some retailers state their times in standard time all year, and the page follows them.
                2. Some plans have tiers. They charge one price for the first part of each day's usage and a different price for the rest.
                3. The daily charge is added for every day.
                4. Your solar exports earn the plan's solar credit.
                5. Percentage discounts come off. The page assumes that you pay on time.
                6. Membership and GreenPower fees are added.
                7. All prices include GST.

                The year ahead

                The list ranks plans on what they would cost you over the next year. It shows each cost as a year, a quarter or a month.

                If you might move or switch again soon, use the Compare page. It also shows each plan's cost over 3 months, 6 months and 2 years. It prices a short stay on the same months of last year, so the season counts.

                Sign-up credits

                Retailers describe sign-up credits in their own words. The page reads the amount, and when it is paid, from these words. Each card quotes the words.

                A credit pays the bill that it arrives on, then the next bills, until it is used up. If you leave the plan before that, you usually lose the rest. So, with "Include sign-up credits in the cost" on, a credit counts only for the bills in the time you stay. In the list, that time is one year. Some examples:

                • A $200 credit on the first bill counts in full if your bills add up to $200 or more.
                • On the Compare page, in a 3-month stay, a credit that arrives on the third bill can pay only the third bill.
                • In a 3-month stay, a credit paid as $25 a month for six months counts as $75.

                Check each credit with the retailer before you sign up.

                Comparing plans

                Press "Compare" on two to four plans. The Compare page then shows them side by side: each part of the cost, and the prices. It has a switch for electricity plans and gas plans; each keeps its own choices. Green marks the best value in each row and the cheapest plan overall.

                A chart shows what you would pay month by month, on last year's usage. It opens on the difference from the cheapest plan that you compare, or from your current plan if you compare it. That plan is the line at $0. Up is always better: a line above $0 saves you money, and a line below $0 costs you more. This makes small differences easy to see. A sign-up credit shows as smaller bills at the start.

                The Compare page also shows the cost of each plan over 3 months, 6 months, 1 year and 2 years.

                Each card's "Rates and details" lists the plan's prices the way its Victorian Energy Fact Sheet does, the standard summary that every Victorian plan must have: including GST, in cents a day and cents per kWh or MJ. Seasons with the same prices share a column. Plan names are tidied a little (spacing, and the network's name at the end); the name as published is there too.

                To compare plans from one retailer, choose the retailer in the list's "Retailer" box. "Best plan from each retailer only" shows the cheapest plan of each retailer. Each card then has a link to see all of that retailer's plans.

                Plans you may not be able to get

                Some plans are only for people with a battery, an electric car, a seniors card, a particular bank or a membership. The page hides these plans until you tick the box for them. Plans for old-style meters also stay hidden until you tick that box, because a usage file comes from a smart meter. If your file shows no solar, plans made for solar homes stay hidden until you switch them on.

                The Default Offer comparison

                The Victorian Default Offer is a price that Victoria's energy regulator sets. Each card shows how much more or less the plan would cost you than the Default Offer of the same kind. The two kinds are same price all day and price varies by time of day.

                Your current plan and switching advice

                Add your current plan: pick it from the list, or type the prices from your bill. This works on the Electricity page and on the Gas page. For gas, the form takes the daily charge and the price, plus two things that many gas bills show: a lower price after the first part of each day's use, and a different price in winter. Each card then shows the difference from your plan. The advice compares a switch now with a stay:

                • You usually lose unused sign-up credit when you leave a plan. So unused credit counts against a switch.
                • A switch now wins if two amounts together are more than the credit you would lose. The two amounts are what you would save before your credit runs out, and any new sign-up credit.
                • If a switch does not win, the page tells you to check again when your credit runs out.
                • The page suggests a switch only if it saves more than $50 a year.

                The page then suggests a date to check again, and can put it in your calendar. If you have unused credit, the date is when the credit should run out: after that, a switch loses you nothing. If not, the date is mid-February or mid-August. Retailers often change prices after 1 July, when the Default Offer changes, so a check every six months catches most changes.

                What is not covered

                • Usage files from CitiPower, Powercor, Jemena and AusNet Services: none has been tried with this page yet. If yours does not load, use a sample home, and please tell me.
                • Plans with demand charges.
                • A sample home with a separate hot-water circuit (controlled load). Your own file with one works.
                • Fees that you can avoid or that you pay only once, such as paper bills, card payments and connection fees. Each card lists them under "Rates and details".
                • Retailers that do not publish to the government list.

                How accurate it is

                I check the pricing against a separate calculation, written in a different programming language. The two agree to the cent on every plan. On my own household's bills, the estimate came within $6 a year. Still, next year will not be exactly like last year, so please treat every cost as an estimate.

                Gas

                The Gas page works like the electricity page, with three differences. Gas has no usage file, so the page works from your bills. Gas prices do not change through the day, so each plan is priced day by day. And the page can show electricity and gas from one retailer.

                Your gas use

                Each gas bill shows the dates it covers and the gas used in megajoules (MJ). From one or two bills, the page builds a year of daily use:

                • The year has the shape of an average Victorian home with gas: little in summer and the most in July, from heating.
                • With one winter bill and one summer bill, the page also splits your use into two parts. One part stays the same all year, such as hot water and cooking. The other part is heating, which follows the average home's rise in winter.
                • With one bill, or two from the same season, the page scales the average home's year to fit your bills.

                A sample home is an average Victorian home with gas, with 1 to 5 or more people. Its use in each season comes from the Australian Energy Regulator's benchmarks (Frontier Economics, 2020). For example, a 2-person home uses about 50,500 MJ a year. The benchmarks give four seasons; the page joins them with one smooth curve through the year, because May is much colder than March. The average includes homes that use gas only for hot water or cooking, so a home with gas heating may use more.

                How each gas plan is priced

                1. Each day of the year costs the plan's gas price for that day's use. Many plans charge a higher price in winter.
                2. Most plans have tiers, such as one price for the first 100 MJ a day and a lower price for the rest. AGL states its tiers for every two months; the page shares them out per day.
                3. The daily charge is added for every day.
                4. Percentage discounts come off. The page assumes that you pay on time.
                5. Membership fees are added, and all prices include GST.

                Gas prices differ by zone within each gas network. Each plan lists the postcodes of its zone, so your postcode picks the plans for your zone.

                Electricity and gas together

                Under the gas plans, the page compares two choices. One is the cheapest electricity plan and the cheapest gas plan, from any retailers. The other is both from one retailer, taking that retailer's cheapest plan of each. Some plans are sold only to homes that take both from one retailer, such as GloBird's Combo gas plans; they count only in the second choice. The ticks and options on both pages apply. One retailer for both is simpler, but it saves money only when it costs less than the cheapest of each. If you have added your current plan on both pages, the page also shows what they cost together, and how much each choice would save.

                Gas limits

                • Your bills give your total use for their dates. How it spreads over the rest of the year comes from the average home, so your own year may differ.
                • A few postcodes sit on the edge of two gas price zones. The page then shows the plans of both zones, and your retailer can tell you which applies.
                • Sharing AGL's two-month tiers out per day can differ from a real bill by a few dollars a year.

                Why this exists

                I made this to make my own life easier. Working out which electricity plan suits our home, from our real usage, took far too long by hand. Once it worked, I put it online in case someone else finds it useful too.

                It is free, and I get nothing from it: no ads, no referral fees and no payments from retailers. It has no link to any retailer, comparison site or government body. Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model by Anthropic, wrote most of the code and the text with me, and I checked the results.

                If something looks wrong or confusing, please tell me through the feedback form.

                Not advice

                Please do not treat anything here as financial advice. It is a comparison tool that I use myself, nothing more, and nothing on it is an offer from a retailer.

                The costs are estimates. They assume your next year looks like your last one, and they use the prices that retailers publish today. Both can change. The page can also get things wrong, for example when it reads a sign-up credit from a retailer's own wording.

                So before you switch, read the plan's Victorian Energy Fact Sheet, and check the prices and conditions with the retailer. The retailer's contract is what you agree to, not this page. For the Victorian Government's own comparison, try Victorian Energy Compare.

                What the page reads from your usage file

                Your browser reads the file right here on your device. It is never uploaded, I never see it, and nothing about it is saved: close the page and it is gone. From the file, the page reads:

                • The date of each day, and how much power your home took from the grid in each 5 or 30 minutes. It uses the last 12 months.
                • How much solar power your home sent out, if any.
                • How much power a separate hot-water circuit used, if your home has one.
                • The first four digits of your meter's ID number (the NMI). Each network's meter IDs start with its own digits, so these four digits tell the page which network the file came from. The page keeps only the network's name. It never keeps, shows or sends the digits or the rest of the ID.

                It skips everything else in the file, such as the meter's serial number and the notes on estimated readings.

                What the page reads from your gas bills

                Only what you type on the Gas page: the dates of one or two bills and the gas used in each. They stay on this device, and the page forgets them when you close it.

                Three levels of privacy

                Everything on this page belongs to one of three levels:

                • On your device only. Your file, your gas bills, your postcode, your answers and every calculation. Nothing from this level leaves your device.
                • Public data only. The page downloads public plan prices. It sends nothing about you. This is the only thing the page does online today.
                • Sent only when you ask. Nothing on the page does this today. If a future feature needs it (for example, reading a photo of a bill), the page will name the service, ask you each time, and do it only if you say yes.

                What goes online

                The page goes online for one thing: public plan prices, for electricity and, when you open the Gas page, for gas. First it downloads this site's weekly copy of the government's plan list, from the same address as the page. If that copy is missing or out of date, or you ask for today's prices, it downloads them from the government's plan list itself (cdr.energymadeeasy.gov.au). Those requests say nothing about you. They do not include your postcode, your usage or your answers.

                To make sure of that, the page tells your browser to block every other address. This is a content security policy. So even if I made a mistake in the code, your data could not go anywhere else.

                What is kept on this device

                Only public data. The price list, for one day, so your next visit is quick. And a short code for each plan's prices from your last visit, so the page can tell you which plans changed since then. The page does not keep your file, your postcode, your ticks or anything you type.

                Clear your data

                The site sets no cookies. Your browser's history will show which pages of the site you opened, such as "How it works", but never your file or your answers. Your browser's settings can clear that history.

                Done. This site keeps nothing on this device now. It keeps prices again the next time that you check plans.

                To remove the kept prices and start fresh, use this button. It also reloads the page, which forgets your file and everything you typed or ticked.

                Links

                Links to retailers and "Search for its fact sheet" links open only when you click them. A fact-sheet search sends DuckDuckGo the plan's ID and the retailer's name, and nothing else.

                "Add to my calendar" makes a calendar file on your device; nothing goes online. The Google Calendar link sends Google only the reminder's date and title.

                The feedback form is a Google Form. It gets only what you choose to type into it, and you do not need to sign in.

                Using it offline

                You can save the page to your computer and open it from there; it works the same way. It needs the internet once, to download prices. After that it runs entirely on your device. With no internet, it uses the last prices it kept and tells you how old they are.

                What is not here

                No accounts, no sign-up, no cookies, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. I do not know who uses it, and I would rather keep it that way.

                Check for yourself

                The whole site is one file of open-source code. You can read it on GitHub, or with "View page source" in your browser.

                Words explained

                Plain meanings for the words on this site.

                Network
                The company that owns the poles and wires in your area and reads your meter. Victoria has five: CitiPower (inner Melbourne), Jemena (north-west Melbourne), United Energy (south-east Melbourne), AusNet Services (eastern Victoria) and Powercor (western Victoria). You cannot choose it. It comes with your address.
                Gas network
                The company that owns the gas pipes in your area and reads your gas meter. Victoria has three: Multinet (east and south-east Melbourne), Australian Gas Networks (north and south-east Melbourne, and many country towns) and AusNet Services (west Melbourne, Geelong, Ballarat and Bendigo). Gas prices also differ by zone within each one. You cannot choose it. It comes with your address.
                Retailer
                The company that sells you electricity or gas and sends your bill. You can choose it, and you can have a different one for each.
                Plan and plan ID
                A retailer's set of prices and conditions. Each plan has an ID, such as OVO803467MR. You can give this ID to the retailer.
                kWh
                A kilowatt-hour, the unit that electricity is sold in. A typical home uses 10 to 20 kWh a day.
                MJ
                A megajoule, the unit that gas is sold in. Your gas meter measures cubic metres of gas; your bill turns them into MJ. An average Victorian home with gas uses about 50,000 MJ a year, mostly for heating in winter.
                Daily charge
                A fixed amount for each day that you are connected, however much you use. Another name for it is supply charge.
                Usage rate
                The price of each kWh that you take from the grid.
                Same price all day
                One usage rate at all hours. Other names for it are flat rate and single rate.
                Price varies by time of day
                Different usage rates at different hours. The highest rate is usually in the late afternoon and evening. Another name for it is time of use.
                Tiers
                Different prices for the first kWh and the later kWh of each day. An example is free power for the first 24 kWh at midday.
                Solar exports
                Power that your solar panels send to the grid when your home does not use it.
                Solar credit
                What a retailer pays you for each kWh of solar power that you send to the grid. Another name for it is feed-in tariff.
                Sign-up credit
                An amount that a retailer takes off your bills when you join. Some retailers pay it at once, and some pay it in parts over several bills. If you leave before it is used up, you usually lose the rest.
                Pay-on-time discount
                A percentage off your bill if you pay by the due date.
                Membership fee
                A regular fee that some retailers charge for a plan, such as Amber's $25 a month.
                GreenPower
                An extra payment so that your retailer buys certified renewable energy for you.
                Victorian Default Offer
                A price that the Essential Services Commission, Victoria's energy regulator, sets each year. It protects people who have never chosen a plan.
                Standing and market offers
                A standing offer is a retailer's basic plan at the Default Offer price. A market offer is any other plan. A market offer usually costs less.
                Smart meter
                A meter that records your use every 5 or 30 minutes and sends the data to your network. Almost every Victorian home has one.
                Old-style meter
                A meter that only keeps a running total. A person reads it on site. Other names for it are basic meter and accumulation meter.
                Usage file
                A download of your smart meter readings from your network's website, as a .csv file. Most networks use a version of the national NEM12 format; Jemena uses its own.
                Controlled load
                A circuit with its own meter, usually for off-peak hot water, with its own lower rate.
                Demand charge
                A charge for your highest use in any half hour of the month, not for your total use.
                Connection and disconnection fees
                Charges to turn the power on when you move in, or off when you move out. Your network sets most of them. They are often free when the network can switch a smart meter from a distance.
                Exit fee
                A fee for leaving a plan early. Most plans have none.
                Consumer Data Right
                The law that makes retailers publish their plans in a standard, public format. This site reads that data.
                Content security policy
                A rule that a web page gives your browser about which addresses the page can contact. This site allows only its own address and the government plan list.
